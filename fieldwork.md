   > *(Translation: If leafy greens like spinach or ripe tomatoes are not sold by 8:30 PM, they wilt by morning. During the last 30 minutes, we are forced to dump them at distress prices or discard them.)*

## 4. One Surprising Observation
> **What Defied My Initial Expectations**:
> I initially assumed inner-lane vendors might sell lower-grade produce because their prices were cheaper. In reality, Shanti Devi's produce was **visibly fresher and firmer** (crisp unbruised spinach, firm ripe tomatoes freshly unpacked) compared to entrance stalls where produce had been sitting exposed to street dust and vehicular exhaust since noon. The fundamental barrier was **not quality or price, but pure spatial information asymmetry**—shoppers at the gate simply had no idea that better and cheaper vegetables were available 40 meters deeper inside.

## 5. Raw Data Collection Method
- **Methodology**:
  - Direct physical observation and inquiry conducted on-site during peak evening market hours.
  - Divided the market into three clear geographical zones:
    1. **Entrance**: Within 15 meters of the main access gate/road.
    2. **Middle Lane**: Between 15 meters and 40 meters from the entrance.
    3. **Inner Lane**: Beyond 40 meters, down narrow alleys or behind permanent shops.
  - Timed 20-minute observational intervals using a stopwatch per vendor to count paying buyers.
  - Inquired directly with vendors for their quoted selling prices per kilogram across staple vegetables.
  - Manually assessed freshness into three transparent categories (`Fresh`, `Moderate`, `Low`) by visual inspection of firmness, skin turgor, leaf hydration, and lack of bruising.

MandiMitra – Problem and User Research

1. Problem Statement

In traditional vegetable markets, vendors located near entrances and main walking paths generally receive more customer attention than vendors positioned deeper inside the market.

Small vendors often struggle to attract customers despite offering competitive prices and good-quality products. Customers tend to purchase from stalls they notice first, leaving less-visible vendors with fewer opportunities.

MandiMitra aims to identify this visibility gap by analyzing vendor locations, product prices, availability, and customer attention patterns.

2. Target Person

Primary User: Small Vegetable Vendors in Local Mandis

The primary target users are small-scale vegetable vendors whose stalls are located deeper inside traditional markets.

Their Problems

- Receive less customer attention due to poor stall visibility.
- Face competition from vendors located near market entrances.
- Have limited opportunities to promote their products.
- Struggle to attract customers even when their prices are competitive.

3. Proposed Solution

MandiMitra is a data-driven vendor visibility analysis tool that helps identify overlooked vendors in traditional vegetable markets.

The system analyzes vendor information, including:

- Vendor location and distance from the entrance.
- Vegetable prices and product availability.
- Customer attention and visibility indicators.
- Differences between highly visible and less-visible vendors.

It generates insights to highlight vendors who may be overlooked and helps understand how market location influences vendor visibility.

4. Initial Hypothesis

Vendors located deeper inside a vegetable market may receive less customer attention than vendors positioned near entrances and high-traffic areas.

By analyzing structured vendor data, MandiMitra aims to uncover these visibility differences and support more inclusive opportunities for small vendors.

5. Expected Impact

- Identify potentially overlooked vendors.
- Improve understanding of visibility inequality in local markets.
- Help customers discover vendors beyond the main entrance.
- Support fairer opportunities for small-scale vegetable sellers.

Research Status: Initial problem hypothesis. Further validation through market observations and vendor data analysis is required.
